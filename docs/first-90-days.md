# The first 90 days with a new customer

This is how I would open a customer as a technical account manager. It is a plan I would put in front of the customer in the first week, not a theory. Every step names the thing you can measure, and Beyond 90 shows that thing, so you are never asked to take the progress on trust.

The customer in the examples is invented. Nothing here describes a real company.

## Days 1 to 10: find out what is really there

Do not start with the product. Start with the customer's own list of what they own.

- Ask for two lists that do not come from the platform: the cloud bill and the organisation or SSO export. If the only list is the one the platform already knows, coverage looks perfect and means nothing.
- Load them and look at the sentence at the top of Today. It tells you how many accounts exist, how many are connected and how far to trust the figure.
- Agree one owner on the customer side for each account. An account with no owner is the first thing that will go wrong.

You are done when both sides agree on how many accounts there are. This sounds small. It is usually the longest conversation of the three months.

## Days 10 to 30: connect and inventory

This is plumbing, and it is where most onboardings quietly stall.

- Work through the accounts that are not connected, starting with the ones that carry the most spend. The onboarding view groups them by the stage where they are stuck, and says who fixes each one.
- Check what is connected but not inventoried. An account that is "connected" and shows no assets is not covered.
- Ask for the integration to be read-only, with the least access the platform documents. Say why. Customers notice who asked.

You are done when every account in scope is connected and shows its assets. Expect the yield to dip first as new accounts come in with their gaps; tell the customer before it happens, not after. The "If nothing is done" view shows the dip with its date.

## Days 30 to 60: switch things on

Connected is not the same as protected. Now go through the capabilities the customer already pays for.

- Take the first three actions on Today, in order. They are ranked by what each one adds, and each has the steps and a "done when" line.
- Pick one team and one use case, not the whole company. A single visible win teaches the customer more than a perfect rollout plan.
- Put the proof of value criteria in writing and get the sponsor to agree them. Five lines: the problem, how we will know, who signs off, the dates, what is out of scope.

You are done when the first use case works end to end and the sponsor has seen it.

## Days 60 to 90: make it theirs

By now the customer should be doing more of the work than you.

- Look at findings past the triage window. Each needs an owner and a decision. If it is accepted, it needs an expiry date.
- Look at how fixes get closed: by hand, with a tool's help, or automatically. The aim is more of the last two over time, and you can show it.
- Write the first executive review. It says where they were, where they are, what changed and what you need from them. Keep it to what a security lead would read in five minutes.

You are done when the customer can tell the story of the quarter without you.

## What I would never do

- Quote a number I cannot explain. Every figure in the tool can show where it came from.
- Promise a date on the customer's behalf.
- Bring real customer data anywhere it should not be. Share with aliases, and remember an alias is not anonymity.
